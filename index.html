SudokuSnap

Privacy policy

Effective 25 August 2026 Applies to SudokuSnap for Android Package com.samjo.sudoku_snap

The short version

  • There is no account, no sign in and no server. The app never asks who you are.
  • Your photographs are read on the phone. They are not uploaded, and the app keeps no copy.
  • Nothing you do in the app is uploaded. One thing does leave the phone, and it is named below.

Who this is from

SudokuSnap is made and published by Sam Jongman. Questions about this policy go to s2550040uni@gmail.com, which is also the contact address shown on the app's Google Play listing.

What the app stores, and where

Everything the app remembers is stored on your own phone, in the app's private storage. None of it is sent anywhere.

  • Your settings. The theme, whether the clock is shown, whether the keys buzz, and how mistakes are marked.
  • The game in progress. The puzzle, the digits and pencil marks you have written, and the time on the clock.
  • Your record. The dates you finished a puzzle on, and how many you have started and finished at each rating, with your times.

None of this identifies you. There is no name, no email address, no account and no device identifier in any of it.

Getting rid of it

Settings has a Clear games and stats control that removes the saved game, the streak and every total immediately. Uninstalling the app removes all of it, settings included. Neither needs a request to anybody, because nobody else has a copy.

Your photographs

When you scan a puzzle, the app takes a photograph with the camera or opens one you pick from your gallery. That image is processed on the phone: the grid is found in it, straightened, and each cell is read by a text recogniser that is bundled inside the app.

The image is held only for as long as that takes. It is not uploaded, it is not copied into the app's own storage, and no part of it is sent to the developer or to anybody else. Picking a photo from your gallery gives the app that one photo, not your library.

What does leave the phone

One thing, and it is not your images.

The text recogniser is Google ML Kit, which runs entirely on the phone but brings Google's own telemetry transport with it. That transport logs usage of the recogniser itself: technical and diagnostic information about the library, the device and the API calls made. It does not send your photographs, the puzzle, your times or anything you have typed.

This is why the app declares the internet permission even though nothing it reads is uploaded. The logging is Google's, it is part of the library, and it cannot be switched off from inside the app. What Google does with it is covered by the Google privacy policy.

The app makes no requests of its own, and adds nothing to what that library sends.

Permissions, and why each one is there

PermissionAsked whenWhat it is for
Camera The first time you tap Camera Taking one still photograph of a puzzle. The app never records video or audio.
Photos The first time you tap Gallery Opening the single photograph you choose. On newer Android versions the system picker hands over one image without giving the app access to the rest.
INTERNET Never asked, granted at install Declared by the ML Kit library for the telemetry described above. The app makes no requests of its own.
ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE Never asked, granted at install Also the ML Kit library's, so it can tell whether it is able to send that telemetry.

The camera library the app uses also declares a microphone permission by default, because it can film. That permission is explicitly removed from this app's manifest: a sudoku app asking for your microphone is both untrue and worth refusing over.

Children

SudokuSnap collects nothing that could identify anyone, of any age. There is no profiling and no way for one player to reach another, so there is nothing here that treats a child differently from anybody else.

Your rights

Under the UK and EU General Data Protection Regulation you have the right to see, correct, export or delete personal data an organisation holds about you. The developer of SudokuSnap holds none: there is no server, no account and no copy of anything from your phone, so there is nothing to hand over or erase on request.

The data described above sits on your device and is under your control at all times, through the Clear control in Settings or by uninstalling. For the telemetry that Google's library sends, Google is the controller, and their policy above sets out how to exercise your rights with them.
